Working Papers

Electronics Manufacturing Clusters and Labor Market Adjustment to the China Trade Shock

Sole authored (2025)  ·  Presented at the 18th WEAI Annual Conference

Abstract

This paper examines whether the concentration of high-tech manufacturing moderates the labor market effects of Chinese import competition across 722 U.S. Commuting Zones over 1990–2019. High-tech commuting zones experienced milder employment and population losses during 1990–2010 and showed signs of recovery by 2019, while low-tech zones exhibited persistent declines in employment, wages, and housing values, with effects concentrated among workers without a four-year degree and among Black, Hispanic, and Asian workers. The findings speak to current debates on place-based industrial policy, including the CHIPS and Science Act of 2022.
