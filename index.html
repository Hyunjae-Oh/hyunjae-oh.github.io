Working Papers

Electronics Manufacturing Clusters and Labor Market Adjustment to the China Trade Shock

Sole authored (2025)  ·  Presented at the 18th WEAI Annual Conference Job Market Paper

Abstract

This paper examines whether the concentration of high-tech manufacturing moderates the labor market effects of Chinese import competition across 722 U.S. Commuting Zones over 1990–2019. Building on the framework of Autor, Dorn, and Hanson (2013), I construct a commuting-zone panel by merging ACS microdata, UN Comtrade trade flows, and Census industry classifications, and implement a shift-share IV design with stacked first differences and cumulative long-difference specifications. High-tech commuting zones experienced milder employment and population losses during 1990–2010 and showed signs of recovery by 2019, while low-tech zones exhibited persistent declines in employment, wages, and housing values, with effects concentrated among workers without a four-year degree and among Black, Hispanic, and Asian workers. The findings speak to current debates on place-based industrial policy, including the CHIPS and Science Act of 2022.

The China Trade Shock Across the Life Cycle: Cohort Evidence from U.S. Commuting Zones

Master's thesis, in progress (2026)  ·  Advisor: Jun Sung Kim Draft available on request

Abstract

This project extends the commuting-zone analysis of the China trade shock to estimate age-group and birth-cohort effects. Using stacked two-period panel IV across 722 commuting zones, I separately identify the shock period (1990–2010) and its aftermath (2010–2019), with the goal of disentangling cohort-specific exposure from contemporaneous age effects and asking whether the costs of trade exposure compound across generations.
